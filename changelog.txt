= 1.8.2 =

A Persian screen gets back the line saying what a Gulf top-up is worth elsewhere.

**Bugfixes**

* On a Persian screen with the region set to GCC, the Credit tab dropped the line saying what an amount is worth in US dollars and euros, and the figure under the amount field stopped following what you typed. The currency's name is translated and its code is not; the two were the same value, so nothing recognised a translated name as Omani rial.

**Other**

**Bugfixes**

= 1.8.0 =

A Support tab, so you can write to DiceX support without leaving your dashboard.

**Enhancements**

* Adds a Support tab after Credit. Its form emails DiceX support with your question, the site's name, address and versions, and, unless you untick it, the plugin's log. The answer comes to the email address you give.

**Security**

**Other**

= 1.7.0 =

One region for the rest of the world, and the fixes from a security review and an accessibility review of the Customer Club.

**Enhancements**

* Folds Europe and the United States into one region, World Wide — the name the product page uses — with WhatsApp and Telegram. A site that chose Europe keeps exactly what it had; a site that chose the United States, which offered nothing, now gets those two channels. Neither falls back to Iran.

**Security**

* An unpaid order no longer counts as proof of who a guest is. A guest's name, email, address and date of birth come from their newest paid order, so an order left unpaid under somebody else's number cannot change what DiceX holds for them.
* Export Personal Data and Erase Personal Data answer only for the requester's own club records: the numbers on their account, and the guest numbers they paid under with that email. Before, an order placed with somebody else's number and your own email was enough to see that person's level and groups, or to take them out of the club.
* An account that types a guest customer's number no longer takes that customer over, unless it has paid under the number itself or carries the email the guest paid with.
* The welcome message goes to an account once, not to every number it types, and never to a number it dropped before it was greeted. A level's message reaches an account at most once in 30 days, however often its own city or date of birth moves it between levels. Only the first 50 characters of a customer's name go into either message.
* The mobile number on a WordPress profile can no longer be one another account already has, since two accounts sharing a number stops both of them signing in with a code. A number already shared stays until it is changed.
* Somebody who leaves the club, or is moved to the level for removed numbers, is from then on sent to DiceX as their number and that level, every other field empty, and the club's tables keep no name, place or date of birth for them.
* Erase Personal Data also clears the details last sent to DiceX, the member's city and country, and the phone and city on the club's copy of their orders, and tells the admin plainly that DiceX keeps whatever it was sent before.
* Uninstalling on a multisite network cleans up every site of it, not only the one the uninstall was started from.
* The error shown when a tab cannot load is written into the page as text, never as markup.

**Accessibility**

* A member's level is changed with an Apply button. Before, arrowing through the list with the keyboard moved the member on every option it passed, and could send them a level's message.
* The order of channels can be changed with up and down buttons, on the Customer Club tab and on every integration card, not only by dragging.
* Keyboard focus stays where it belongs when a level, group, city or product is removed, when a panel closes, after the first import is sent or a refusal retried, and while a button is busy: a busy button now says so instead of being disabled.
* Screen readers are told what happens: an import finishing, the first send waiting, sending paused, a count starting and its result, what a search found, a member moved, a city already listed, a level's new place. The member list is no longer redrawn under somebody working in it.
* Each condition is a named group, so "From" and "To" are heard with Purchases, Orders, Days since the last purchase or Age, and their unit. Filter boxes, panels and each level's buttons say which list or level they belong to, and a subcategory says which category it is in. Escape closes a list.
* The "In another language" buttons no longer open an empty conditions panel, or lose their count of written languages.
* A group's state on the member list has a mark and words of its own, not only a colour, and a member held in a level by hand says so to a screen reader.
* Tags, chips and error text meet WCAG AA contrast, the switch has a visible edge when off, and the level arrows are at least 24 pixels square.
* Every date of birth field carries autocomplete="bday", and the WordPress registration form says in words how to write the date.

**Bugfixes**

* Moving a member by hand no longer hides their groups until the list is loaded again.
* The admin numbers say "1 number stored" and "1 number saved", not "1 numbers", and translations get a plural form of their own.

**Notes**

* Nothing needs doing after the update. The first sync afterwards sends the members who left or were removed once more, without their details.
* All eight screenshots on the plugin page are new, taken from 1.7.0.
* The plugin page, its banner, the description on the Plugins screen and the Getting started screen now describe the whole plugin: the Customer Club first, then the messages for your site's events. The tags follow: customer club, crm, sms, whatsapp and woocommerce.
* The plugin is now called DiceX Connect – Customer Club and Event Notifications. Only the name changes: its address on WordPress.org, its folder and its settings stay as they were, and nothing needs doing.

= 1.6.0 =

Stops checkouts and forms waiting on DiceX, and gives the Customer Club a command line.

**Enhancements**

* Sends the integration cards' messages — orders, forms, security alerts — after the visitor's page has gone out, the way WordPress runs its own scheduled tasks: the response is finished first where the server allows it, so a checkout or a form no longer waits on the gateway. Nothing is stored to be sent later, and a message that fails is logged and not sent twice. Login codes, Digits and the test button still send at once, since somebody is waiting for them. A site that needs the old behaviour can return false from the `dicex_connect_send_after_response` filter.
* Adds `wp dicex club` for WP-CLI: `status` shows members, what is waiting, the levels and the groups; `import` reads customers and works through them with the club's own rules, waiting for release after the first import as the screen does; `sync` works through everything waiting; `retry` puts refused customers and memberships back in the queue; `release` sends the first import once you have checked it.
* On a store, the ready-made levels now open with Inactive: customers who have not bought in 180 days, checked first, for win-back campaigns.

**Bugfixes**

* WordPress 6.7 and later no longer logs "Translation loading for the dicex-connect domain was triggered too early" on every request — a notice that a site with debugging on also saw at the top of every wp dicex command. The integration cards now start on after_setup_theme, still before anything they listen to happens.

**Notes**

* The readme gives the Customer Club a section of its own, and its list of what is sent now names the contact groups and the club's messages, which 1.4.0 added.

= 1.5.0 =

Lets the club ask how old a customer is.

**Enhancements**

* Adds an age condition to levels and groups, from and to, in whole years. It is counted from the customer's date of birth in the calendar they read — Solar Hijri in Persian, Gregorian otherwise — because 21 Mordad is 12 August in some years and 11 August in others, and a customer turns a year older on their own birthday, not a day beside it.
* Moves customers on their birthday by itself, in every sync mode, as the daily run already does for days since the last purchase. A day the site slept through is caught up on the next run.
* Counts ages in Count customers before anything is saved.
* Works without WooCommerce too: a WordPress user's date of birth counts the same as a customer's.
* Somebody born on 29 February is a year older on 28 February in a common year, and somebody born on 30 Esfand on 29 Esfand — the birthday stays in the month it belongs to.
* Says on a level or group when nobody can meet its age condition because the club asks for no date of birth.

**Notes**

* The club's own copy of each date of birth is kept only while something needs it — DiceX, or an age condition — and Erase Personal Data removes it.
* Birthday greetings are DiceX's: its panel sends them from the date of birth the club already sends, so the plugin sends none of its own.
* Existing members are looked at once after the update to record their dates of birth. Nothing is sent to DiceX because of it.

= 1.4.0 =

Groups your customers by what they buy, alongside the levels, and writes to them in their own language.

**Enhancements**

* Adds contact groups. A group has the same conditions as a level, but a customer is in every group they match instead of one, so you can gather, say, everybody who buys coffee and everybody who buys from Tehran and reach each of them in DiceX. A group needs at least one condition, and nobody in the level for removed numbers is put in one.
* Counts customers for the groups too, before anything is saved, and shows each group's size, how many memberships are still on their way, and how many contacts no longer match.
* Says on each member's row which groups they are in.
* Sends a message to a customer who moves up into a level, when you write one for that level. Only upwards, never on joining — the welcome covers that — and never for the level kept for removed numbers. It goes over the same channels as the welcome message.
* Lets you write the welcome message and each level's message in any language the site is read in. Each customer is written to in the language of their account, or the one they were reading when they ordered, with the plain text as the fallback.
* Creates each group in your DiceX account as it is first needed, and reuses a group of the same name that is already there.

**What DiceX cannot do**

* Nothing takes a contact out of a group: the DiceX API has no such call. A customer who stops matching a group stays in it at DiceX, and both the group and the member's row say so.

= 1.3.0 =

Asks customers for their date of birth and whether they want to be in the club, and lets a level ask how long ago somebody last bought.

**Enhancements**

* Asks for the date of birth at checkout, classic and block, on the WordPress and WooCommerce registration forms, in the customer's account and on the WordPress profile screen. It is optional unless you make it required, and goes to DiceX with the year.
* Takes a date of birth typed in the Solar Hijri calendar or the Gregorian one, in Persian, Arabic-Indic or Latin digits. Persian visitors see a Shamsi example; every date is kept and sent as Gregorian. The conversion needs no PHP extension, and matches the official calendar for every year from 1206 to 1498.
* Reads birthdays another plugin already keeps, from a user meta key you name.
* Lets you choose who joins the club: every customer, or only customers who tick a box at checkout, when registering or in their account, with the date and the place of that choice recorded. There is no default; the club cannot be switched on before you choose. A club that is already on keeps taking everybody in.
* Lets customers leave the club from their account, in both modes. They move to the level for removed numbers at once, and ticking the box again brings them back. The level for removed numbers is now needed before the club can be switched on.
* Adds a condition on the days since the last purchase, from and to. A customer who never bought anything does not meet it. The daily run moves people as those days pass, now in every sync mode.
* Adds Count customers to the levels: how many customers each level would hold with the levels on screen, and how many would move, before anything is saved.
* Holds the first import once it has read your customers and placed them in levels. Nothing reaches DiceX until you have seen the counts and pressed Send to DiceX.
* Covers the date of birth and the club choices in WordPress's Export and Erase Personal Data tools, and in the suggested privacy policy text.

**Bugfixes**

* In the "Only as things happen" sync mode, purchases leaving the counting window now lower customers' totals; the daily run used to skip that in this mode.

= 1.2.0 =

Lets a club level ask what a customer bought and where they buy from.

**Enhancements**

* Adds five conditions a level can use besides spend and orders: bought from product categories, where a subcategory counts too; bought products; bought from brands; the buyer's country; and the buyer's city. Inside one list any single entry is enough, and every condition a level sets must hold.
* Matches cities however customers typed them. Capital letters, spaces and the Arabic or Persian forms of a letter are ignored, and you can list every spelling you want to count.
* Looks at a product's buyers again when the product moves to another category or brand.
* Shows each member's city and country in the members list.
* Knows a registered customer by the phone on their newest order when their account has none saved.

**Bugfixes**

* Saving club settings while WooCommerce is switched off no longer clears the purchase conditions of your levels.

**Other**

* A site that installed 1.1.0 reads its orders once more after this update, so the new conditions apply to past purchases too.

= 1.1.0 =

Adds the Customer Club: your WordPress users and WooCommerce customers, registered in your DiceX club, in levels you define.

**Enhancements**

* Adds a Customer Club tab. You name your own levels, put them in order, and give each a purchase range in your store currency and a range of order counts. A customer joins the first level whose conditions they meet, and the level you choose for everybody else when they meet none.
* Moves customers between levels by themselves. A new order, a refund or a cancellation is noticed as it happens, and a daily run catches anything missed and lowers totals when only recent purchases count.
* Imports your existing customers in one go, with a progress bar. Guests who paid for an order join by their billing phone, completed with their billing country's code when they typed it without one.
* Lets you put a member in a level by hand, where they stay until you hand them back to the conditions. That is how a number leaves the club: DiceX deletes nothing, so it moves to the level you keep for removed numbers.
* Adds an optional welcome message, sent once when somebody joins, over the channels you choose — never to customers brought in by an import.
* Covers the club in WordPress's Export and Erase Personal Data tools, and adds a suggested paragraph to the privacy policy guide.
* Names the plugin, WordPress, PHP and WooCommerce versions, the site language, the region and the site address in the User-Agent of every request to DiceX.

**Other**

* Sends nothing during a customer's checkout. Changes are recorded and sent in the background, fifty customers to a request, and a customer DiceX refuses does not hold anybody else back.

= 1.0.25 =

Finishes the lockout fix that 1.0.24 only half closed.

**Bugfixes**

* Fixes a sign-in still being refused when the code could not reach you. A mobile number written in the Iranian national form, with its leading 0, lost that zero outside Iran and became a number with no country code that nothing could deliver to — so the message never arrived and the login was refused with it.
